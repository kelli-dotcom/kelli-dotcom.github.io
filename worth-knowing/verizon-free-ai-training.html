Bring your project. Leave everybody’s private information out.

I want you to use what you learn. Just keep the boundary clear. Use material you created or have permission to use. Remove anything another person trusted you to protect. This is practical guidance, not legal advice.

  • Leave private data out of public AI tools. That means no student records, customer details, passwords, internal files or confidential business information.
  • Use work you have rights to use. Do not copy a protected article or course and publish it as your own. The same goes for images and brand assets.
  • Check important facts. AI can invent sources, numbers and other details. Verify anything that could affect another person’s money, safety, work or decisions.
  • Follow the rules where you work. Your school or employer may have stricter rules than the tool itself. A client may too.

Turn the free course into proof

A certificate shows that you completed the training. A small finished project lets an employer or client see what you can actually do with it. Put the two together, and the course becomes part of your story instead of one more line on a list.

The training is free, so the real question isn't whether to start. It's what you'd do with the certificate once it's yours. Job hunt, a promotion case, or just proof you can learn the thing?

A simple 3-part learning record

  • Course. Write down the exact title and provider, along with the completion date.
  • Skill. Name one practical thing you learned to do.
  • Proof. Keep a safe, non-confidential example or short project that shows the skill in action.

A finished project ties the course to your goal, whether that’s a job change or better work where you are.
