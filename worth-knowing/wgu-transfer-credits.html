I should say it plain: I haven’t gone to college, and I’m not enrolled at WGU. I’m earning free and cheap credits first, so when I do enroll, the degree takes less time and costs less money.

While researching a WGU bachelor’s path in cloud computing, I started looking for credits I could finish for free or at a lower cost before enrolling. That is when I found the part I almost missed: finding a course is not enough. You have to make sure the college accepts it, that your exact degree accepts it, and that you finish the transfer process on time.

The timing matters more than most people realize.

WGU’s published policy says transfer credit is not awarded after a student’s initial term start date. Its transfer page also says official transcripts and certifications should arrive by the fifth day of the month before the planned start date. Do the transfer-credit work before your first term begins, not after.

A course can be accepted by a school and still not fill a requirement in your degree.

That difference is where people can lose time and money. “This college accepts credits from this provider” is only the first check. The real question is: Which exact course does this exact program use, and what requirement does it replace?

Not enough

“WGU accepts credits from this provider.”

What you need

“This course currently satisfies this requirement in my exact WGU program.”

How to search without wasting money

  1. Choose the school, degree and track

    Do not stop at “an IT degree.” Write down the exact program and specialization. WGU now lists the related bachelor’s program as Cloud and Network Engineering, with General, AWS, Azure and Cisco specializations.

  2. Use the college’s official transfer pages

    Start with the receiving college—not a social-media list. WGU’s transfer resources include program guidelines and instructions for third-party providers, certifications, credit by exam and prior college work.

  3. Match course to requirement

    Record the provider, course name, course code, number of credits or competency units, and the degree requirement it may satisfy. Check the date because mappings can change.

    For a general-education example, start with Sophia Learning’s official site and write down the exact course before you compare it.

  4. Confirm before paying

    Ask an enrollment counselor to explain anything unclear. WGU does not provide unofficial transcript evaluations, so the official evaluation is what finally decides what transfers.

    If that example is Sophia Learning, confirm the current WGU mapping before you pay.

  5. Finish and send official records

    A completion badge or screenshot is not the same as an official record. Follow the provider’s instructions to send the transcript or certification directly to WGU, and leave time for processing.

  6. Read the evaluation before starting

    Make sure the credits landed where you expected. WGU’s catalog says applicants may transfer up to 75% of a program, but the actual maximum and course fit are determined by the program.

Where free or lower-cost credits may hide

The best option is not always a new class. Start by checking what you already earned, then look outward.

Certificates you already completed

Industry certificates, including some technology credentials, may meet admission or transfer requirements depending on the program and how recently they were earned.

For Google’s options, check the Google IT Support Professional Certificate and Google Project Management Professional Certificate pages, then compare any completed courses with the official transfer mapping.

Check first

Prior college coursework

Order records from every school you attended—even if you did not finish a degree. A course-by-course evaluation may clear requirements.

Already earned

Workforce and community programs

Libraries, workforce agencies, employers and nonprofit training programs sometimes cover certificates or college-level learning. The American Dream Academy was my route, but that program ended in 2024, so do not treat old sign-up links as current.

May be free

Approved third-party course providers

WGU’s transfer page specifically names Sophia, Study.com and StraighterLine as third-party options. Prices and accepted courses change, so check the official mapping before subscribing.

Often lower cost

Credit by exam

WGU’s general guidelines note options including AP, IB, CLEP and DANTES. Eligibility depends on your score, program and current policy.

Pay per exam
For any college—not just WGU: search the school’s official transfer-credit page, then narrow to the exact degree. One school may accept a provider while another does not, and two degrees at the same school can use the same credit differently.

The five-line check before you pay

Put this in your Notes app for every course you are considering:

  1. My target school: the college receiving the credit.
  2. My exact program: degree plus specialization or track.
  3. The outside course: provider, title and course code.
  4. The requirement it should satisfy: copied from the current official mapping.
  5. The deadline and delivery method: when and how the official record must arrive.

That small check can keep a “cheap” course from becoming wasted money. I am still banking credits before I enroll, but this changed how I look at college planning: the opportunity is not only finding affordable learning. It is making sure that learning moves you toward the degree you actually want.
